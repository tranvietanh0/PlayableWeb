## Executive Summary

Hong Kong's Private Company Limited by Shares structure (Cap. 622) offers Vietnamese founders a uniquely advantageous framework combining **territorial taxation** (profits outside HK = 0% HK tax), **no currency controls**, and **low regulatory compliance cost**. The One Game Studio Ltd. benefits from an 8.25% tax rate on first HK$2M of HK-sourced profits (significantly below Vietnam's 20% and US federal 21%). The HK-Vietnam Double Tax Avoidance Agreement (2010) eliminates double taxation on dividends and allows repatriation to Vietnam with minimal withholding. Audited financial statements are mandatory (no exemption threshold), and annual filings via NAR1 cost HK$105 when on-time (42-day deadline). Operational cost is ~HK$25-70K annually including corporate secretarial services, audit, and statutory fees. The structure is ideal for multinational game studios leveraging HK's low tax, free capital movement, and strategic position between Asian and Western markets.

## Section 1: Hong Kong Companies Ordinance Cap. 622 Essentials

### 1.1 Overview of Cap. 622 and the 2014 Reform

The Hong Kong Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622), which superseded the Companies Ordinance 1932 (Cap. 32), came into force on **3 March 2014**. This marked the first major overhaul of Hong Kong's corporate law in 80+ years and introduced the **no-par-value share concept**, making Hong Kong one of only three Commonwealth jurisdictions with this feature (alongside Australia and New Zealand).

**Key reform objectives:**
- Modernize corporate governance aligned with common law jurisdictions
- Reduce regulatory burden on private companies
- Codify certain fiduciary duties of directors
- Introduce SCR (Significant Controllers Register) framework post-2018

[Full text: Cap. 622 Companies Ordinance, Hong Kong e-Legislation](https://www.elegislation.gov.hk/hk/cap622)

### 1.2 Private Company Limited by Shares: Definition and Restrictions

A private company limited by shares is defined under **Cap. 622 § 103(1)** as a company where:

1. **Share transfer restrictions** — its articles restrict members' right to transfer shares
2. **Member cap** — maximum **50 members** (shareholders)
3. **No public offering** — cannot invite the public to subscribe for shares or debentures

**The One Game Studio Ltd.** complies with all three: articles include transfer restrictions, has 5 shareholders, and operates without public subscription.

**Consequences of the private company structure:**
- No prospectus requirements
- Simplified reporting exemption available (though audit remains mandatory)
- Less prescriptive on board/AGM procedures vs. public companies
- Qualified for simplified directors' statement under Cap. 622 § 409

[Key changes under Cap. 622 — Charltons Law](https://www.charltonslaw.com/hong-kong-law/key-changes-under-the-companies-ordinance-cap-622/)

### 1.3 No-Par-Value Share System

**Prior regime (Cap. 32):** Shares had nominal/par value (e.g., HK$1 per share). Authorized capital was published and fixed.

**Current regime (Cap. 622 § 157-159):** **All shares are no-par-value.** Founders declare total number of shares to issue and amount to pay up, but there is no "authorized but unissued" concept.

**Practical implication for The One Game Studio Ltd.:**
- Shares are defined by rights (voting, dividend, liquidation preference) not by par value
- Preferred vs. Ordinary distinction is governed by Articles, not by share class par value
- Pricing for new issuance is determined by Board resolution + Member resolution, with no par-value floor

### 1.4 Required Officers

#### 1.4.1 Directors

**Minimum:** At least **1 director** (can be sole director)

**Eligibility:**
- Individual or corporate director permitted
- **At least one natural person director required** (corporate director alone is insufficient per Cap. 622 § 379)
- Director **may be non-HK resident** (visa not required; common for overseas founders)
- No age minimum if sound mind; age 18+ recommended

**The One Game Studio Ltd.:** Hoang Anh Tu (Founder, resident Vietnam) is sole director + 47.1% trustee position. Structure is compliant.

**Duties (Cap. 622 §§ 465-478):**
- **Fiduciary duties** — act in good faith in interests of company, exercise powers for proper purpose, avoid conflicts
- **Duty of care** — exercise reasonable care, skill, diligence per Cap. 622 § 465
- **Personal liability** — directors can be held personally liable for breach; shadow directors have equivalent duties per § 545

[Guide on Directors' Duties — HK Companies Registry](https://www.cr.gov.hk/en/companies_ordinance/docs/Guide_DirDuties-e.pdf)

#### 1.4.2 Company Secretary

**Requirement:** Every company must appoint **1 company secretary** (Cap. 622 § 380)

**Eligibility:**
- **Individual:** Must be aged 18+ and **ordinarily resident in Hong Kong** (principal place of residence is HK, not just work visa)
- **Corporate:** Must be a HK-registered company or overseas company registered in HK, holding TCSP (Trust and Company Service Provider) licence issued by Companies Registry

**Constraint:** If company has only 1 director, that director **cannot simultaneously be company secretary** (requires separate person/entity)

**The One Game Studio Ltd.:** Company secretary is **GLAC (Global Link Asia Consulting, Singapore)** — a licensed TCSP firm. GLAC is registered in HK and maintains a principal place of business meeting Cap. 622 requirements. This is standard practice for foreign-owned private companies.

**GLAC scope (per contracts repo):** Annual returns (NAR1), financial reporting, share allotments, particulars change notices, audit coordination.

[Company Secretary Requirements — HK Companies Registry 2026](https://www.bestar-hk.com/post/hong-kong-company-directors-and-secretaries-the-ultimate-2026-compliance-guide)

### 1.5 Registered Office in Hong Kong

**Requirement:** Every HK company must maintain a **registered office in Hong Kong** per Cap. 622 § 114.

**Options:**
1. Physical office owned/rented by company
2. Office of licensed TCSP firm (most cost-effective for private companies)
3. Registered address provided by corporate registry service provider

**The One Game Studio Ltd.:** Registered office is **Unit 2202, 22/F., Causeway Bay Plaza I, 489 Hennessy Road, Causeway Bay, HK**. This is GLAC's business center address, common arrangement costing ~HK$2-5K annually.

**Requirement to notify:** Any change to registered office address must be filed via **NSC1 (Notice of Change of Registered Office)** form within **10 days** per Cap. 622 § 115.

### 1.6 Articles of Association

**What are articles?** Internal rules governing how the company operates, including member rights, director powers, share transfers, AGM procedures.

**Customary sections for 2-class private company (Ordinary + Preference):**
1. Share structure — classes, voting rights, dividend rights, liquidation preference
2. Transfer restrictions — consent of directors, tag-along/drag-along rights
3. Director powers — appointment, removal, remuneration, vacation of office
4. Meetings — AGM frequency, voting, written resolutions, quorum
5. Distributions — dividend policy, interim vs. final, capital reduction procedures
6. Dispute resolution — arbitration clause (recommended)

**HK Companies Registry provides Model Articles** per Cap. 622 Schedule 1; many private companies adopt these with customizations.

**The One Game Studio Ltd.** — articles should be reviewed to confirm:
- Preferred share rights (dividend priority, liquidation preference per investor terms)
- Ordinary share voting rights (1 vote per share or pro-rata by class)
- Transfer lock-up periods or consent gates (common for founder shares)
- Pre-emptive rights language (protect existing shareholders on dilution)

### 1.7 Public Disclosure: Shareholder Confidentiality Trade-off

**Major difference from US states (Delaware, Wyoming):**

HK shareholder list is **publicly searchable** via paid e-search on HK Companies Registry website (~HK$10 per record). Unlike Delaware (where beneficial owners are confidential), HK discloses:
- Names of shareholders
- Number of shares held
- Class of shares (Ordinary vs. Preference)

**Privacy mitigation strategies:**
1. **Nominee structure** — use corporate nominee company (owned by trustee) as shareholder; beneficial owner remains confidential. **Caution:** Adds complexity, requires nominee agreement.
2. **Trust structure** — place shares in irrevocable trust; trustee is named shareholder. Beneficial owner confidential. (The One Game Studio Ltd. uses trustee model for Hoang Anh Tu's 47.1% holding.)
3. **Accept public disclosure** — many founders accept this trade-off as cost of HK incorporation.

**SCR (Significant Controllers Register):**
Introduced 2018 per Cap. 622 § 377, every company must maintain internal register of anyone holding **>25% voting power or significant influence**. This register is **not public** and reviewed only during company inspections or regulatory investigations.

[Key changes under Cap. 622 — Charltons Law](https://www.charltonslaw.com/hong-kong-law/key-changes-under-the-companies-ordinance-cap-622/)

## Section 2: Inland Revenue & Tax Regime

### 2.1 Territorial Taxation: The Profit-Zone Advantage

Hong Kong's **Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112)** applies a **territorial basis of taxation**. Unlike the US (which taxes citizens and residents on worldwide income) or Vietnam (which taxes residents on worldwide income above VND 3 billion threshold), **HK taxes only profits arising in or derived from HK**.

**Formula:**
```
HK Tax = (HK-Sourced Profits only) × Tax Rate
Profits from offshore activities = 0% HK tax
```

**Practical implication for The One Game Studio Ltd.:**
If dev team is in Vietnam, publishing services delivered in US/Europe, and HK entity holds IP license: profits attributable to Vietnam dev costs + overseas sales are potential candidates for **offshore profit exemption**. This is the dominant strategic advantage of HK incorporation for game studios.

[Hong Kong Corporate Tax — Statrys 2026](https://statrys.com/guides/hong-kong/tax-system-and-rates/hong-kong-corporate-tax)

### 2.2 Profits Tax Rates (Corporations)

#### Two-Tier Rate Structure (in effect from 2018 onwards)

| Profit Band | Rate | Applies to |
|---|---|---|
| First HK$2,000,000 of net assessable profits | **8.25%** | All corporations |
| Above HK$2,000,000 | **16.5%** | Remaining profits |

**Unincorporated businesses** pay 7.5% and 15% respectively (not applicable to The One Game Studio Ltd., which is incorporated).

**Per-Group Election:** If multiple companies are in a "related group," only **one entity** can claim the lower 8.25% rate; others use the 16.5% rate. Group must file election with IRD.

**Example:** The One Game Studio Ltd. (HK) + PlayableLabs Inc. (US) — because PlayableLabs is US-incorporated, not HK, the group election does not apply. Each files separately in its jurisdiction.

[Hong Kong Profits Tax — IRD Official](https://www.ird.gov.hk/eng/tax/dta_rates.htm)

### 2.3 Offshore Profit Claim: The Zero-Tax Pathway

If **all profit-generating activities occur outside HK** (sales negotiation, product delivery, service execution in overseas jurisdictions), the company may claim **exemption from HK profits tax**, resulting in **0% HK tax**.

**Qualifying factors (IRD Departmental Interpretation and Practice Note 21 — DIPN 21):**
1. **Contracts signed overseas** — not in HK
2. **Services delivered overseas** — work performed by overseas team, not HK team
3. **Payments received overseas** — funds received outside HK financial system (though immediate transfer is acceptable)
4. **No HK PE** — no permanent establishment in HK performing core income-generating activities

**Documentation required:**
- Contemporaneous records showing contract location, delivery jurisdiction, where revenue recognition occurred
- Transfer pricing documentation (if related-party transactions exist)
- Working papers for auditor review

**Risk:** IRD scrutinizes offshore claims. If evidence is weak (e.g., HK director negotiated contract, HK team managed project), IRD may challenge and assess HK-source profits at 8.25% / 16.5%.

**For The One Game Studio Ltd.:**
If (a) development team is Vietnam-based, (b) publishing contracts signed in Vietnam/US, and (c) revenue delivered to international game stores (Apple, Google, Steam): strong factual basis for offshore claim. **Recommend:** Document this in Board resolution and auditor working papers before first Profits Tax Return filing (typically May after FYE 30 June).

[Offshore Profit Claim — PKF Hong Kong](https://www.pkf-hk.com/services/tax-services/hong-kong-tax/making-an-offshore-profit-claim/)

### 2.4 Profits Tax Filing and Timing

**Annual Profits Tax Return (BIR51):**
- **Issued by IRD** — typically in March/April after FYE
- **Due:** Within **1 month of issue** (strict deadline, rarely extended)
- **FYE:** 30 June (for The One Game Studio Ltd.)
- **Return filed:** Typically May/June following FYE

**Components of return:**
1. Net profits calculation (revenue less deductible expenses per Cap. 112 § 16)
2. Assessable profit after allowances and reliefs
3. Tax payable (net assessable profit × applicable rate)
4. Provisional tax reconciliation

**Two-year provisional tax system:**
- **Current year tax** = actual tax for current FYE, payable in one lump sum upon Notice of Assessment
- **Provisional tax** for next year = 1/2 estimated next-year tax, paid on same date (unless taxpayer objects within stipulated time)
- Next year actual = adjusted on final assessment; overpayment credited or refunded

**Example (FYE 30 June 2025):**
- BIR51 issued May 2025
- Final tax for FY2024-25 + provisional tax for FY2025-26 due by ~June 30, 2025
- Once FY2025-26 finalizes, provisional paid in June 2026 is credited toward actual; refund/top-up issued

[BIR51 Guide — IRD](https://www.ird.gov.hk/eng/faq/faq_bit.htm)

### 2.5 Mandatory Audited Financial Statements

**No exemption threshold.** All HK companies must prepare audited financial statements, regardless of size or turnover, **except dormant companies**.

**Requirements (Cap. 622 §§ 386-399):**
1. **Prepared by licensed HK CPA** (HKICPA) or Big 4 firm equivalent
2. **Scope:** Balance sheet, P&L, cash flow, notes to accounts
3. **Standards:** HKFRS (Hong Kong Financial Reporting Standards) or IFRS
4. **Auditor report:** Unqualified, qualified, adverse, or disclaimer opinion
5. **Due:** Lodged with Companies Registry **within 4 months after AGM** (AGM must be held within 9 months of FYE)

**Estimated cost (2025/26):**
- Simple game studio (low transaction volume): **HK$15K - 25K/year**
- Medium complexity (related-party transactions, foreign subsidiary): **HK$30K - 50K/year**
- Large studio with multiple subsidiaries: **HK$60K+/year**

**Dormant company exemption:** Company with no accounting transactions, approved by special resolution, and notified to Companies Registry. Not applicable to The One Game Studio Ltd. (active game development).

**Reporting exemption (simplified accounts):** Some companies may prepare simplified P&L (not full balance sheet), but **audit requirement remains**. Rarely relevant for active studios.

[Audit Requirements — HK Companies Registry](https://www.cr.gov.hk/en/compliance/annual-return/private-company.htm)

### 2.6 Deductible Expenses

Per **Cap. 112 § 16**, expenses deductible against assessable profits include:

- **Ordinary business expenses** — salaries, rent, utilities, travel
- **Professional fees** — audit, legal (capital-raising or restructure excluded), accounting
- **Development costs** — staff costs for game development (art, coding, design)
- **Publishing costs** — App Store fees (up to 30%), advertising, localization
- **Bad debts** — written-off debtor balances (with conditions)
- **Capital allowances** — depreciation per Cap. 112 § 37, limited to designated assets

**Non-deductible:**
- Dividends paid to shareholders
- Income tax provisions
- Capital expenditure (added to balance sheet, depreciated)
- Penalties, fines
- Donations (not gifts with condition; charitable donations allowed under Cap. 112 § 88D)

**Related-party transaction scrutiny:** If paying Vietnam dev team, overseas contractor, or affiliate company, IRD requires arm's-length pricing. Underpayment (transfer pricing manipulation) results in assessment + penalties.

### 2.7 Tax Treaties: Hong Kong-Vietnam Double Tax Avoidance Agreement (2010)

The **Hong Kong-Vietnam DTA (Comprehensive Agreement for the Avoidance of Double Taxation), signed 1 September 2010, effective from 1 January 2011**, is critical for The One Game Studio Ltd. given founder residency in Vietnam.

**Key withholding tax rates under HK-Vietnam DTA:**

| Income Type | Without Treaty | Treaty Rate |
|---|---|---|
| **Dividends** | 0% (no WHT in HK) → Beneficial in Vietnam per DTA | 10% (standard) |
| **Interest** | 0% (no WHT in HK) | 10% (standard) |
| **Royalties (IP)** | 4.95% (HK law) | 7% (patents, designs); 10% (other) |
| **Gains on share disposal** | 0% (capital gains not taxed in HK) | Generally exempt if not substantial investor |

**Vietnam side (sourced gains taxed in Vietnam):**
- **Dividend WHT:** 5% to 20% depending on holding period and dividend type (Vietnam Decree 69/2020 amended rates)
- **Participation exemption:** Gains on sale of Vietnam subsidiary >25% ownership taxed at reduced rates

**Mechanics for Vietnamese-resident shareholders:**
1. HK dividend remitted from The One Game Studio Ltd.
2. HK imposes **0% WHT** (no withholding tax on dividends under HK law)
3. Vietnam taxes shareholder dividend income per Vietnam PIT law (5%-20% depending on holding period)
4. DTA Article 10 sets maximum withholding at 10% if both countries impose withholding; HK's 0% is more generous

**Practical implication:** Hoang Anh Tu (Vietnam resident, Founder/CEO) receives dividends from HK company with no HK withholding; Vietnam tax-resident status means Vietnam PIT applies on dividend income.

**CRS Reporting Consequence:** HK financial institutions (Airwallex, Payoneer) report HK account information for Vietnam tax residents to Vietnamese tax authorities under OECD CRS framework (Vietnam joined CRS in 2020). This means dividend payments + account balances are reported to Vietnam tax authority automatically. Tax planning should account for expected Vietnam PIT liability.

[HK-Vietnam DTA Rates — IRD](https://www.ird.gov.hk/eng/tax/dta_rates.htm)
[Vietnam Double Tax Avoidance Agreements — Vietnam Briefing](https://www.vietnam-briefing.com/doing-business-guide/vietnam/why-vietnam/vietnam-double-tax-avoidance-agreements)

### 2.8 No Withholding Tax on Dividends (HK Law Baseline)

Hong Kong law imposes **0% withholding tax on dividends**, interest, and most capital gains, regardless of recipient residence.

This is markedly different from the **US (30% withholding on foreign dividends unless treaty applies; US-Vietnam treaty does not exist)** and creates a significant structural advantage for HK-incorporated companies with Vietnam-resident shareholders.

**No WHT on:**
- Dividends declared and paid to any shareholder (0% HK WHT)
- Interest paid to non-resident creditors (0% HK WHT)
- Capital gains on share sales (0% HK tax on gains)

**WHT applies only on:**
- Royalties paid to non-resident by HK company (4.95% of royalty; Cap. 112 § 49), reduced to 7% / 10% under treaties
- Payments to non-resident contractors or offshore entities for services (questionable withholding in some cases; consult tax advisor)

[Withholding Taxes in Hong Kong — Wise](https://wise.com/gb/blog/hong-kong-corporate-tax)

### 2.9 Common Reporting Standard (CRS) Implications

Hong Kong is a participating jurisdiction in the OECD's **Common Reporting Standard (CRS)**, effective from **1 January 2017** under the **Inland Revenue (Amendment) (No. 3) Ordinance 2016**.

**What CRS requires:**
- All HK financial institutions must identify account holders resident outside HK for tax purposes
- Report account information (name, address, tax ID, account balance, interest/dividend income) to HK Inland Revenue Department
- HK IRD automatically exchanges this data with tax authorities of 120+ reportable jurisdictions, including **Vietnam** (CRS participant since 2020)

**Practical impact for The One Game Studio Ltd.:**
- Bank accounts at **Airwallex** (HK-regulated fintech) will trigger CRS reporting if account holder is Vietnam tax-resident
- Hoang Anh Tu (Vietnam resident) + company account will be reported to Vietnamese tax authority
- Vietnam tax authority receives: account balances, dividend flows, interest income
- Use this for forward tax planning; declare dividend income in Vietnam PIT filing

**No privacy protection:** Account information is exchanged automatically without account holder approval. This is standard post-2017 for all HK-regulated financial institutions.

[CRS Overview — PwC Hong Kong](https://www.pwchk.com/en/services/tax/common-reporting-standard.html)

### 2.10 Stamp Duty on Share Transfers

When shares in The One Game Studio Ltd. are transferred (sold, gifted, or pledged), **stamp duty** is payable on the transaction.

**Current rate (as of November 2023):**
- **0.2% (reduced from 0.26%)** of the higher of:
  - Actual consideration paid, OR
  - Net asset value of shares transferred

**Additional fixed fee:**
- HK$5 per transfer document

**Example:** Angel investor (Phan Trong Bach) purchases 32.2% Preferred shares for HK$500,000:
- Stamp duty = 0.2% × HK$500,000 = HK$1,000
- Plus HK$5 fixed fee = **Total: HK$1,005**
- Payable: Within 2 days of local sale or 30 days for offshore transaction

**Filing:** Stamp duty documents filed with IRD within stipulated time; failure to affix stamp results in 5× the stamped duty as penalty.

**Exemptions:**
- Share transfers between group entities (holding company to subsidiary, subject to conditions)
- Gifts to spouses or children (no consideration = no stamp duty)

**Planning note:** For founder share lock-ups or vesting schedules, ensure share transfer documents clearly state: "0 consideration" (gift to vested employee) vs. explicit price (transfer with value).

[Stamp Duty Rates — GovHK](https://www.gov.hk/en/residents/taxes/stamp/stamp_duty_rates.htm)

### 2.11 No VAT / GST / Sales Tax

Hong Kong has **no Value Added Tax, Goods & Services Tax, or Sales Tax**, even on international sales. This is a major simplification advantage vs. EU (VAT 15-27%), US (sales tax 0-10.75% by state), or Vietnam (VAT 10%).

For game studios exporting to global markets:
- No VAT on app purchases, in-game revenue, or game licensing
- Invoicing is simplified (no "reverse charge" mechanisms)
- Pricing is cleaner (publish HK$99 game = HK$99 revenue, no additional tax pass-through)

## Section 3: Annual Compliance & Corporate Secretary Functions

### 3.1 Annual Return (NAR1): The Critical Deadline

The **Notice of Annual Return for Local Company (Form NAR1)** must be filed with the Hong Kong Companies Registry within **42 days after the anniversary of the company's incorporation date**.

**Calculation for The One Game Studio Ltd.:**
- **Incorporation date:** 24 June 2024
- **Anniversary:** 24 June each year
- **NAR1 deadline:** 5 August (42 days after 24 June; note: Sundays and public holidays are counted, but if 42nd day falls on Sunday/public holiday, deadline extends to next business day)

**Contents of NAR1 (as of made-up date, e.g., 24 June 2025):**
1. Registered office address
2. Directors — names, HK address, nationality, NINO/HKID, occupation, appointment date
3. Company secretary — name, address, qualification (licensed TCSP or HK-resident individual)
4. Shareholders — names, addresses, number of shares held, class of shares
5. Share capital — total authorized, total issued, paid-up capital
6. Significant controllers (SCR) — any person holding >25% voting power or significant influence

**Filing method:** Electronic submission via **ECNC** (E-Channels) — Hong Kong Companies Registry's online filing system. GLAC typically handles this on behalf of the company.

**Filing fee (on-time):** HK$105

**Late filing penalties (2025/26):**
| Late period | Penalty |
|---|---|
| 1-3 months late | HK$870 |
| 3-6 months late | HK$1,740 |
| 6-9 months late | HK$2,610 |
| 9+ months late | HK$3,480 |

**Example:** If NAR1 not filed by 5 August, and filed on 15 September (43 days late):
- Penalty = HK$870 (falls in 1-3 months late bracket)
- Total cost = HK$105 (normal fee) + HK$870 (penalty) = **HK$975**

**No discretion to waive:** Registrar of Companies cannot waive late fees. Prevention is far cheaper than cure.

**Practical note:** GLAC should set internal reminder 45 days after incorporation anniversary to prepare NAR1. The One Game Studio Ltd. (24 June FYE) should have NAR1 ready by late July each year.

[Annual Return — HK Companies Registry](https://www.cr.gov.hk/en/compliance/annual-return/private-company.htm)

### 3.2 Business Registration Certificate (BR)

**Separate from NAR1.** Every business operating in HK must hold a valid **Business Registration Certificate** under the **Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310)**.

**Details required on BR:**
- Name of business
- Principal place of business (Causeway Bay Plaza address)
- Nature of business (game development and publishing)
- Registered proprietor (The One Game Studio Ltd.)

**Renewal:** **Annually, on anniversary date** (for The One Game Studio Ltd.: 24 June each year, same as incorporation anniversary).

**Fee (2025/26):** HK$2,200 (increased from HK$2,150 in prior year)

**Filing:** Paper form BR 1 or electronic submission. Often bundled with NAR1 filing through GLAC.

**Failure to renew:** Business operating without valid BR is **illegal**; penalties up to HK$5,000 and criminal prosecution possible (though rarely enforced for companies with agents).

[Business Registration — HK Inland Revenue Department](https://www.ird.gov.hk/eng/tax/br.htm)

### 3.4 Companies Registry Particulars Changes (NSC1 / NSC2 / NR1 / NAA1)

When any material information changes between annual returns, **notification forms** must be filed with Companies Registry within **10 days**.

**Common change notices:**

## Section 4: Capital Raising & Shareholder Governance

### 4.1 The No-Par-Value Share System Revisited (Capital Raising Context)

Under **Cap. 622 § 157-159**, shares in HK companies have **no nominal (par) value**. This simplifies certain aspects of fundraising but requires clear documentation.

**Implication for share issuance:**
- No par-value floor or ceiling on share price per class
- New Preferred shares can be issued at any price decided by Board + Members (no par-value constraint)
- Dilution is purely dependent on voting power / economic rights, not par value

**Example scenario (The One Game Studio Ltd. Series A):**
Suppose Phan Trong Bach (current 32.2% Preferred investor) participates in Series A at higher valuation:
- Series A: New investor buys 25% of post-money equity at HK$5M valuation
- New shares issued: whatever number achieves 25% post-money (e.g., if company worth HK$5M pre-money + HK$2M investment = HK$7M post = new investor gets ~14.3% of total shares)
- Price per share: HK$2M investment / number of shares = price (no par-value floor applies)
- All existing shares remain no-par (price determined retrospectively by economic reality)

### 4.2 Share Allotment Process & Companies Registry Filing

When new shares are issued (e.g., Series A funding, employee stock option plan), the following process is required:

**Step 1: Board Authorization**

### 4.3 Pre-emptive Rights: Essential for Founder Protection

**HK law does NOT automatically grant pre-emptive rights.** Unlike UK Companies Act 2006 (automatic pre-emptive rights unless opted out), HK companies must **explicitly include pre-emptive language in Articles**.

**Why it matters:** Without pre-emptive rights, investor board seat + new funding round can result in massive founder dilution without founder consent.

**Example (The One Game Studio Ltd. — worst case without pre-emptive rights):**
- Current cap table: Hoang Anh Tu 47.1%, Phan Trong Bach 32.2%, others 20.7%
- Series A fundraise: HK$5M investment, new investor gets 25% post-money equity
- New investor has Board seat, proposes Series B at unfavorable valuation
- Series B: HK$10M for 33% post-money equity (implies HK$20M post)
- **Without pre-emptive:** Hoang Anh Tu has no right to maintain pro-rata stake; dilution compounds
- **With pre-emptive:** Hoang Anh Tu can purchase pro-rata share in Series B to maintain ~47% stake

**Standard pre-emptive rights language (recommended addition to Articles):**

```
### Pre-emptive Rights (Sample Language)

(1) If the Company proposes to allot any new shares, the Directors shall 
first offer those shares to existing shareholders in proportion to their 
current shareholding in the Company.

(2) Offer shall be in writing, specifying:
    (a) Number of shares offered
    (b) Price per share
    (c) Closing date (minimum 14 days from offer date)

(3) Existing shareholder must notify Directors of acceptance/rejection 
within closing date. Unaccepted shares may be allotted to third parties.

(4) Pre-emptive rights do NOT apply to:
    (a) Employee share schemes (discretionary)
    (b) Shares issued pursuant to employee options exercised
    (c) Shares issued in a stock split or bonus issue
```

**For The One Game Studio Ltd. (recommendation):**
Before Series A fundraise, file **NAA1 (Notice of Amendment to Articles)** adding pre-emptive rights section. Cost: ~HK$500 filing fee + ~HK$2-3K legal review. **Do this now, before investor board seat is granted.**

[Pre-emptive Rights — UK Companies Act Parallel (HK practice is similar despite no statutory requirement)](https://www.charltonslaw.com/hong-kong-law/key-changes-under-the-companies-ordinance-cap-622/)

### 4.4 Share Transfer Mechanics & Restrictions

**Private company Articles must restrict share transfers** (to maintain "private" status per Cap. 622 § 103). Standard restrictions include:

**Director consent requirement:**
- Shareholder proposing to transfer must seek written consent from Board
- Board may withhold consent in its discretion (absolute discretion vs. reasonable grounds varies by Articles)
- Typical provision: "No share shall be transferred unless consent of Directors has been obtained; Directors may refuse without stating reasons"

**Tag-along rights (protective for majority):**
- If majority shareholder sells >50% stake to third party, minority has right to sell pro-rata stake at same price/terms
- Typical: "If holder of 50%+ voting power sells all or substantially all shares, minority shareholders have right to sell their shares at same price"

**Drag-along rights (protective for majority):**
- If majority shareholder (e.g., 75%+) sells company, minority can be forced to sell at same price/terms
- Typical: "If holders of 75%+ voting power approve sale of company, minority shareholders shall be obligated to sell at same price"

**Lock-up periods (common for founder shares):**
- Founder cannot sell shares for fixed period (e.g., 3 years post-funding) except with investor consent
- Example: "Ordinary shares held by founders cannot be transferred for 3 years from Series A close date without written consent of investors holding >50% Preferred voting power"

**Right of first refusal:**
- If shareholder wishes to sell to third party, company has right to purchase at same price before third party can buy
- Less common than tag-along/drag-along but protects company ownership continuity

**For The One Game Studio Ltd.:**
- Current Articles likely restrict transfers with Board consent (standard private company provision)
- **Before Series A:** Request investor review of Articles transfer restrictions. Investors typically demand:
  - Tag-along rights for minority protection (rarely disputed)
  - Drag-along rights for majority (founder may negotiate threshold, e.g., 80% vs. 75%)
  - Possible founder lock-up (1-2 years common for game studios to demonstrate retention)

### 4.5 Convertible Instruments & SAFE Notes

For early-stage fundraising, founders may issue **convertible notes** or **SAFE** (Simple Agreements for Future Equity) rather than immediate share issuance.

**Convertible note (common in HK):**
- Loan instrument that converts to Preferred shares on trigger event (Series A funding, acquisition, IPO)
- Interest rate typically 5-8% p.a.
- Maturity: 2-3 years; if no trigger event, note is repayable in cash
- Discount on conversion: 15-30% (note holder gets shares at discount when Series A occurs)
- Valuation cap: Optional ceiling on share price at conversion (if company valued >cap at Series A, conversion uses cap price)

**Example (PlayableLabs pre-Series A):**
- Issue HK$500K convertible note to seed investor
- Terms: 6% interest, 2-year maturity, 20% conversion discount, HK$2M valuation cap
- If Series A at HK$5M valuation: note converts to shares at effective price based on 20% discount (not HK$5M valuation, but ~HK$4M)
- If no Series A by 2 years: investor can demand repayment of HK$500K + accrued interest (~HK$60K)

**SAFE note (uncommon in HK, more US-centric):**
- Non-debt instrument; avoids interest accrual and maturity date
- Converts on triggering event (Series A, acquisition, IPO, dissolution)
- No interest, no maturity; investor gets shares (or cash) only on trigger
- Popular with US angel investors; less familiar in HK legal context

**HK tax treatment (Inland Revenue Ordinance § 16):**
- Convertible note interest is deductible business expense for company
- Conversion does not trigger immediate tax event (no recognition on conversion to equity)
- If note is forgiven or written-off, may trigger taxable gain per Cap. 112 § 20

**For The One Game Studio Ltd.:**
If issuing convertible notes in current raise cycle:
- **Recommended structure:** Convertible note under HK common law (not SAFE, unfamiliar to HK courts)
- **Concise agreement:** 2-3 pages specifying: principal amount, interest rate, conversion triggers, valuation cap, discount, maturity date, default provisions

- **Cost:** ~HK$3-5K for bespoke convertible note term sheet

### 5.3 Transfer Pricing: Inter-Company Services

Regardless of structure (sister or holding), any transaction between HK and US entities must follow **arm's-length principle**:

**HK side:** Inland Revenue Ordinance § 50AAF (Transfer Pricing Documentation)  
**US side:** Internal Revenue Code § 482 (Transfer Pricing Regulations)

#### 5.3.1 IP Licensing Agreement (HK → US)

**Scenario:** HK entity grants exclusive license to PlayableLabs (US) to develop, publish, and monetize specific game titles in US territory.

**Key terms:**
1. **Royalty rate:** Fixed % of US gross game revenue (10-25% typical for game IP)
   - Comparable: Royalty rates in game industry range 10-20% for proven IP, 15-25% for new IP with development support
   - IRO § 50AAF requires transfer pricing file documenting comparable rates
2. **Territory:** US only (non-exclusive for rest of world; allows HK to license to other publishers in Asia)
3. **Term:** 5 years with renewal option
4. **Exclusivity:** US entity has exclusive right to publish; HK retains IP ownership
5. **Development cost allocation:** HK entity bears game development costs; US entity bears localization, marketing, publishing costs

**Example calculation:**
- PlayableLabs (US) generates HK$5M game revenue in FY1 from HK game IP
- Royalty rate: 15% of revenue
- Royalty due from US to HK: HK$750K
- HK entity records: IP Licensing Revenue HK$750K (included in assessable profits, subject to HK 8.25% tax if offshore claim denied)
- US entity records: IP Licensing Expense HK$750K (deductible on US tax return, reducing US taxable income)

**Transfer pricing documentation required (BEPS Action 13 — per IRO § 50AAF and IRS § 482):**
1. **Master File:** Description of group structure, key IP, intangible asset profile, key business drivers
2. **Local File:** Detailed transfer pricing analysis for HK entity, showing:
   - Functional analysis (what functions HK performs: IP development, maintenance, enforcement)
   - Comparable uncontrolled prices (CUPs) from third-party IP licensors
   - Economic analysis supporting 15% royalty rate as arm's-length

**Audit risk:**
- HK IRD may challenge royalty rate as too low (argue HK is transferring too much profit to US)
- US IRS may challenge royalty rate as too high (argue US subsidiary is unfairly burdened)
- Consensus view: 15-18% is defensible for game IP with modest brand equity; <10% or >25% triggers audit risk

**Recommended approach:** Engage transfer pricing specialist (Big 4 firm: Deloitte, EY, KPMG TP practice) to prepare TP documentation. Cost: HK$15-30K for multi-jurisdiction TP file. Essential if audited or if PlayableLabs generates >HK$10M annual revenue.

- **Profits Tax Return (BIR51)** per Cap. 112 § 51
- **Assessable profits** = HK-sourced income + deemed income
- **Deductions:** Direct costs (dev team salaries, software licenses) + allocated overhead

**Impact of US sister (PlayableLabs):**
- If HK entity licenses IP to PlayableLabs: HK records IP Licensing Revenue HK$750K (from example above)
- HK taxable profits = (HK-sourced revenue) + (IP Licensing Revenue) - (deductible expenses)
- If offshore claim is filed: Argue that IP Licensing Revenue is not HK-sourced (if US entity developed / marketed game, royalty should be 0% HK tax). **Risk:** IRD may disagree.

**Better practice:** Segregate HK-source profits (Hong Kong-based activities only) separately from deemed-revenue categories (IP licensing may be debatable). Conservative approach: Pay HK tax on 50% of IP licensing revenue to reduce audit risk.

#### 5.4.2 US Entity Tax Returns (PlayableLabs Inc.)

**PlayableLabs Inc. files:**
- **Form 1120** (US Federal Corporate Income Tax Return) annually
- **State tax return** (Delaware does not have corporate income tax; but if PlayableLabs has employees in California / New York, would file state returns there)
- **Assessable income** = Game sales revenue - Direct costs (Apple/Google fees) - IP licensing expense (HK) - Operating expenses

**Effective US tax rate:**
- Federal: 21% (corporate rate)
- Apple/Google fee: 30% (not a tax, but revenue reduction; e.g., HK$1M gross = HK$700K after fees)
- Example: If PlayableLabs generates HK$5M gross game revenue:
  - After Apple/Google: HK$3.5M
  - Minus IP Licensing to HK: HK$525K (15% of gross)
  - Minus operating expenses: HK$1M
  - Net profit: HK$1,975K
  - US federal tax @ 21%: ~HK$414K
  - **Effective tax on gross: 414 / 5,000 = 8.3%**

**Comparison: HK + US combined tax burden** (sister structure):
- HK entity: HK$750K royalty received × 8.25% = HK$61.9K
- US entity: HK$1,975K profit × 21% = HK$414.8K
- **Total: HK$476.7K on HK$5M revenue = 9.5% combined rate**
- Contrast: If all HK-source: 8.25% on HK$5M = HK$412.5K. **But US marketing/sales are HK-source if delivery in US**, so HK would claim offshore, resulting in ~0% HK tax and only US 21% tax on HK$5M = HK$1,050K. **So sister structure is actually advantageous** (9.5% vs. 21%).

### 5.5 Permanent Establishment Risk

**US:**
If HK employees work for PlayableLabs Inc. from HK office, could create **PE (Permanent Establishment) in Hong Kong**. Definition per US-HK tax treaty (N/A — no treaty; look to common law):

**Risk scenario:**
- HK entity has 2 developers who spend 50% time on PlayableLabs (US) game project
- HK office is visible PublicPlayableLabs workspace
- IRS could argue: PlayableLabs has "fixed place of business" (HK office) through which substantial activity is conducted
- If PE found: PlayableLabs' income attributable to HK office could be subject to HK tax (via deemed branch)

**Mitigation:**
1. **Clear employer-of-record designation:** Specify in IP License + Management Services agreements: "HK entity is employer of HK-based staff. HK staff provides services only to HK entity; any work for PlayableLabs is through intercompany service agreement paid at arm's-length."
2. **Independent contractor alternative:** PlayableLabs contracts with HK entity for development services, rather than direct employment; avoids appearance of PlayableLabs PE
3. **Segregate functions:** HK developers work on HK-source games + HK Publishing (if applicable); US developers work on PlayableLabs US Publishing
4. **Documentation:** Board resolutions, employment contracts, timesheets showing allocation of effort

**HK:**
Less risk, as HK has no employees (just directors + GLAC secretary). If PlayableLabs has no physical presence in HK, no PE concern.

### 5.6 Currency Management

**HK:** Pegged to USD at HK$7.8 (official peg range HK$7.75-7.85). De facto, HK$ tracks USD; no currency risk between HK and US operations.

**Exposure:**
- Most operating expenses in HK are local (rent, audit, GLAC fees) = HK$ denominated
- Most revenue in US is USD (Apple, Google pay in USD)
- Minor VND exposure if Vietnamese dev team is paid in VND (currently ~VND 24,000/USD)

**Strategy:**
- Keep operating cash in USD (Airwallex, Wise USD account)
- HK$ float just for local expenses; convert USD to HK$ on demand (monthly, not daily) to minimize FX fees
- Vietnam dev costs: Pay in VND if available; if not, negotiate USD rates with Vietnam team (avoids VND→HK→USD conversion slippage)

### 6.1 Summary of Strategic Advantages (HK Jurisdiction)

The One Game Studio Ltd. benefits from:

1. **Territorial taxation (dominant advantage):**
   - 8.25% on first HK$2M of HK-source profit (vs. Vietnam 20%, US federal 21%)
   - 0% tax on offshore profits (game development in Vietnam, sales in US)
   - Combined HK + US structure: ~9-10% effective rate on global profits (vs. 20%+ in home country)

2. **No withholding tax on dividends:**
   - 0% HK WHT on dividends to shareholders (vs. US 30%, Vietnam 5-20%)
   - HK-Vietnam DTA reduces Vietnam PIT on dividend income
   - Founder can repatriate profits efficiently

3. **Capital movement freedom:**
   - No foreign exchange controls (contrast Vietnam's SBV approval requirement)
   - Currency convertibility unrestricted (HK$ pegged to USD)
   - Airwallex, Payoneer can move funds in/out freely

4. **English common law + stable rule of law:**
   - HK companies ordinance transparent, updated regularly (2014 overhaul modern)
   - Court system respected; dispute resolution via arbitration or HK courts (English law precedent applies)
   - Contract enforcement reliable

5. **Low regulatory compliance burden (vs. public companies):**
   - No mandatory Board committee requirements (audit, compensation, nominating)
   - Private company can use written resolutions (avoid AGM costs)
   - Simplified reporting exemption available (though audit mandatory)
